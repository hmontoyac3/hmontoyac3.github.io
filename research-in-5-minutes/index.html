Paper oneFinal review, with Arnaldo Camuffo and Thomaz Teodorovicz

Measuring scientific decision-making in organizations

Journal
Research Policy, research note
Question
Can we measure scientific decision-making with a short survey?
Data
Validated five ways · 3,178 managers · 54 firms · Italy and Brazil

Why a measure

  • Camuffo et al.'s RCTs show that founders trained to decide like scientists perform better.
  • But each study scores reasoning with its own instrument and trained assessors.
  • And managers inside established firms are almost unstudied.
  • So: can a short survey measure it, comparably, across managers and firms?

Years of building it

  • 2022 to 202312 interviews with senior managers → a pool of 49 items
  • 2023Internal and lab review; experts in five countries; a 16-expert pilot
  • 2023Three randomized pre-test waves: 462 managers, six of eight firms agree to field it
  • 2023 to 2026Field deployment: 3,178 managers, 54 firms, Italy and Brazil

The result: 24 items on Causal Reasoning, Evidence Evaluation and Adaptive Learning. The field data exist thanks to a long effort by my coauthors and the team.

Does it measure what we want?

Five tests, and each one could have failed.

All this effort already shows two patterns worth a closer look, and opens many questions.

Pattern A · Managers, not firms

≈ 2%

Share of the variation between firms (ICC 0.018). The differences lie between colleagues in the same firm.

Pattern B · One practice tracks hierarchy

What it opens

  • Pattern B: who reaches the top, or what the top demands?
  • Why do colleagues in the same firm decide so differently?
  • Can training move managers, as it moves founders?
  • Does it relate to how firms innovate?
Paper twoIn analysis, with Alfonso Gambardella

Who benefits from thinking like a scientist?

Data
6 RCTs · 1,184 early-stage startups · 3 arms
Model
Generic ML inference (Chernozhukov, Demirer, Duflo & Fernández-Val, Econometrica 2025)
Outcomes
9 tested: sales, selling at all, Theory and Empirics scores, effort, team size, exit
North
Still under discussion

Why it matters

  • Deciding like a scientist raises founders' performance on average.
  • Camuffo et al. 2020 (+8 papers) ↗
  • The open question: for whom?

The data

  • 6 RCTs: Netherlands, Italy, Colombia, UK, Spain, India
  • 1,184 early-stage startups
  • Control · Evidence · Theory + Evidence
  • About 14 months of follow-up

The model

  • Generic ML inference: Chernozhukov, Demirer, Duflo & Fernández-Val, Econometrica 2025
  • Lets us identify who gains most from the training, with valid inference
  • 250 splits · 7 learners · 212 specifications

36 moderators, in six families

The founder gender · degree · STEM · business studies

Experience work · managerial · industry · startups founded · prior training

Beliefs chance of quitting or pivoting · how innovative the idea is and could be

Scientific approach before training theory · hypotheses · evidence · evaluation · decision

The venture stage · months operating · full-time · sales at baseline

Cognition illusion of control · planning horizon · perceived uncertainty · team

The questions it answers

  • Does the training work differently across founders?
  • How large is the gap between who gains most and least?
  • Which traits are over-represented among those who gain most?
What we see

Knowing who gains the most in sales tells us nothing about who learns the most.

The training's effect on sales climbs from the bottom fifth to the top fifth. Its effect on the Theory Score stays flat across the same founders.

  • Tested formally, with the method's own inference: the sales score does not predict learning gains (0.01 ± 0.05).
  • It holds with the richer covariate set, with a different learner, and with the overall score.
  • Heterogeneity is detectable in 7 of the 9 outcomes we test; exit is the exception.

What we can say so far

Does theory add value beyond evidence?
Yes: more sales, and more founders sell.
Does everyone gain the same?
No: gains are uneven in 7 of 9 outcomes.
Do the founders who gain most in sales also learn most?
No. Their learning gains are no larger.
Who learns without selling?
Early-stage, less experienced founders. Experienced founders at later stages sell without learning.

Questions that came up

Do those who learn without selling quit more?
No (+4 pp, not significant).
Do they start selling later?
Not so far, though few founders remain in the last round.
Does the sales effect hold in levels (Poisson)?
Same direction, weaker.

Still to test: how we split the sample · why the disconnect happens · Theory + Evidence against Control.
