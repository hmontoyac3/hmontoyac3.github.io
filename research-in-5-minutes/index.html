Paper twoIn analysis, with Alfonso Gambardella

Who benefits from thinking like a scientist?

Data
6 RCTs · 1,184 early-stage startups · 3 arms
Model
Generic ML inference (Chernozhukov, Demirer, Duflo & Fernández-Val, Econometrica 2025)
North
Still under discussion

Why it matters

Teaching founders to build a theory and test it raises sales on average. Nobody has mapped for whom; moderators are tested one at a time.

The data

Six RCTs in the Netherlands, Italy, Colombia, the UK, Spain and India. Control, Evidence, and Theory + Evidence training, followed for about fourteen months.

The model

Machine learning predicts each founder's gain on half of the sample, and the other half checks it. 250 splits, 7 learners, 212 specifications.

A careful start

0.605 · 0.607

The CGJ appendix code and the Econometrica code, replicated on our data, give the same average effect.

Thirty-six moderators

Founder and venture traits, each tied to published theory: experience, cognition, beliefs, stage.

Outcomes

Monthly sales, selling at all, Theory and Empirics scores, effort, team size, and exit.

Theory + Evidence, compared with Evidence only

+1.91log points of monthly sales

for the fifth of founders who gain the most (p = .002).

The fifth who gain the least: about zero. Average: +0.55. The pattern holds across covariate sets, groupings, 140 variants and both implementations.

What we can say so far

  • Theory adds sales beyond evidence alone, and more founders sell.
  • The gain is concentrated: the top fifth gains a lot, the bottom fifth nothing.
  • Experienced founders are over-represented among those who gain most.
  • Both trainings teach founders to use evidence and to quit more (+8 pp compared with control).

Next: choosing the north

  • Who benefits: from a portrait to a theory of why.
  • Absorbing and selling: are those who absorb more theory the ones who sell more?
  • Robustness: the spread against repeated observations of the same firm.

To be decided with Alfonso, then into the draft.
